## 1. Anatomy and function of the GI system components; normal intestinal flora
**Partial**

Supported components: The digestive tract moves material from mouth to anus while accessory organs supply secretions. Mucosa, submucosa, muscularis and outer covering provide distinct layers. Villi and microvilli enlarge the absorbing surface; the large intestine recovers water and electrolytes. Acid, bile, movement and antibodies help shape the microbial distribution. The liver processes absorbed nutrients; the gallbladder stores and concentrates bile; the exocrine pancreas supplies enzymes and alkaline fluid.

## 4. Understand the process of digestion
**Partial**

Supported components: Digestion combines mechanical mixing with chemical breakdown. Salivary and pancreatic amylases process starch; pancreatic proteases act on proteins; emulsification separates fat droplets before lipolysis. Sugars and amino acids enter villus capillaries; absorbed lipid products leave through lacteals. Segmentation mixes contents against the surface, whereas peristalsis propels them.

## 6. Know dumping syndrome: pathophysiology, etiology, prevention, clinical manifestations, diagnostics, treatment and complications
**Partial**

Supported components: After partial gastrectomy, a bariatric procedure or pyloroplasty, loss of reservoir and emptying control allows hypertonic chyme to enter the intestine rapidly. Early symptoms include cramps, diarrhea, fullness and nausea. Late symptoms at one to three hours include weakness, sweating and confusion. The supplied dietary strategy uses small frequent meals, more protein and fewer carbohydrates, and fluids between meals.

Missing/partial components: Formal diagnostic criteria, full osmotic/hormonal mechanism, prevention before surgery and detailed complication management are absent.

## 9. Know different types of diarrhea-causes

Lactase deficiency illustrates the osmotic route: unabsorbed lactose contributes to diarrhea and gas.

## 12. Know intestinal obstruction: pathophysiology, etiology, prevention, clinical manifestations, diagnostics, treatment and complications
**Partial**

Supported components: Distinguish a lesion blocking the bowel from functional failure of motility. Adhesions are a common small-bowel cause; large-bowel causes include cancer, volvulus and strictures. Obstruction level changes the pattern of distention and vomiting. In children, congenital narrowing, malrotation, Hirschsprung disease and intussusception provide different mechanisms. Telescoping bowel can compress its mesentery, obstruct venous return and progress to ischemic injury.

## 13. Know bilirubin: what is it? What is the bilirubin cycle?
**Partial**

Supported components: Bilirubin comes from breakdown of aged red blood cells. Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid soluble; hepatocytes conjugate it with glucuronic acid, making it water soluble (slide65 notes). Intestinal bacteria convert downstream bilirubin products to urobilinogen. Jaundice can reflect excessive production, hepatic processing disease or obstruction of bile flow.

Missing/partial components: Full transport, recycling and excretion fractions are absent. Slide64 pigment simplification needs textbook clarification before detailed cycle testing.

## 16. Know GERD in adults and children: pathophysiology, etiology, prevention, clinical manifestations, diagnostics, treatment and complications

## 24. Know anatomy of bone, know bone cycle
**Direct**

Supported components: Bone combines collagen-based tensile strength with mineral rigidity. Osteoblasts form osteoid, osteocytes coordinate maintenance and osteoclasts resorb bone. RANKL promotes osteoclast activity; OPG acts as a decoy. Remodeling alternates activation, resorption and new formation. Fracture repair proceeds through hematoma, procallus, callus, replacement and remodeling.

Nutrition, general health, growth hormone and growth regulators influence the process.
