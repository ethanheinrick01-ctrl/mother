## 15. Clinical manifestations of pressure ulcers — Direct

Unrelieved pressure occludes capillary flow and causes ischemia and necrosis. Stage I has nonblanchable redness with intact skin; stage II partial skin loss; stage III full skin loss reaching subcutaneous tissue; stage IV damage to muscle, bone or supporting structures. A slough-covered base is unstageable; deep red or purple intact skin can signal deep tissue injury. Repositioning, pressure relief and moisture control address contributing forces.

## 17. Types of psoriasis — Direct

Psoriasis is a relapsing T-cell-mediated proliferative disorder. Epidermal turnover shortens from 26–30 to 3–4 days, leaving inadequate time for maturation. Plaque is most common; inverse is rare; guttate is more common in children; pustular develops pus; erythrodermic produces widespread red scaling. Arthritis and nail disease are listed systemic complications. Treatment ranges from emollients and topical agents to phototherapy and systemic agents according to severity.

## 27. Burns all levels in adults and children-pathophysiology, definitions, clinical manifestations, determination of severity, calculation of body surface burned and management, complications — Partial

## 43. MODS-patho, definition, clinical manifestations management — Direct

MODS involves at least two failing organ systems after severe illness or injury. Primary MODS follows the direct insult; secondary MODS reflects an amplified host response, often after another insult. Primed leukocytes, endothelial damage, permeability changes, blood-flow maldistribution and a net procoagulant state impair organs. Confusion, lung dysfunction and renal/hepatic failure are supplied manifestations. Prevention controls the trigger and avoids second insults; support includes oxygenation, nutrition, volume restoration and organ-specific support.

## 47. What is penetrance? Expressivity? Dominance? Recessiveness? — Direct

Penetrance measures the fraction with a genotype who show its expected phenotype. Expressivity describes variation in the phenotype. Dominance means one allele's effect is visible with another present; recessiveness means that effect is hidden in the heterozygote. A carrier can transmit a disease allele while remaining phenotypically normal.

## 48. Understand incidence, prevalence, risk — Direct

Incidence counts new cases over a specified period in a population. Prevalence describes the affected fraction at a point in time. Relative risk divides incidence in an exposed group by incidence in an unexposed group. A snapshot of existing disease cannot substitute for the rate of new cases.
