Vesicoureteral reflux reverses the normal direction of urinary flow. Begin at the bladder and identify where each ureter enters.

The normal junction helps prevent urine from moving backward during bladder emptying.

In reflux, a congenital junction abnormality or abnormal ureteral insertion can allow urine to travel back toward the ureter and kidney. Watch the direction change along the same anatomy.

When the bladder urine is infected, reflux can carry that infection upward and encourage repeated pyelonephritis.

The supplied chapter describes silent cases as well as recurrent urinary infections, unexplained fever, and poor growth. It lists prompt treatment of infection and intervention for recurrent or high-grade reflux. This visualization shows the direction and consequence of reflux.
