An obstruction changes what happens upstream. In this urinary tract illustration, urine normally moves from the renal pelvis through the ureter toward the bladder.

Now a narrowing interrupts that outflow. The supplied chapter distinguishes hydroureter, dilation of the ureter, from hydronephrosis, dilation of the renal pelvis and calyces.

Watch the collecting spaces expand upstream of the obstruction. Severity depends on its location, completeness, duration, and whether one or both upper tracts are involved. Prolonged damage can produce fibrosis and loss of nephrons.

When obstruction is relieved, increased urine output may follow. The chapter calls this postobstructive diuresis and warns of fluid and electrolyte imbalance.

This film does not supply the missing explanation of how unilateral obstruction causes hypertension. That requested guide component remains explicitly marked as a textbook gap.
