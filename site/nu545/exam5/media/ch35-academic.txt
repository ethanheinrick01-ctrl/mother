Alveolar injury changes both the barrier and the airspace.

In the exudative phase of acute respiratory distress syndrome, damage increases permeability of the alveolar and capillary membrane. Fluid crosses into the lung, and surfactant becomes inactivated. Watch the airspace shrink as fluid accumulates. Oxygen exchange is now impaired by more than a shortage of inspired air.

The next view shows repair and proliferation. The supplied chapter describes type two pneumocytes, fibroblasts, and myofibroblasts during this phase.

In a fibrotic phase, remodeling and alveolar destruction further disrupt the exchange surface. These are source-described stages, with overlapping time ranges, not an inevitable clock for every patient.

Keep this process separate from bronchial asthma: asthma narrows the conducting airway through smooth muscle contraction, swelling, and mucus. ARDS damages the alveolar and capillary exchange region.
