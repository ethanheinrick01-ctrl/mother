Follow the filtrate through the loop of Henle. Its two limbs run beside each other, but they do not handle water and solute in the same way.

The supplied chapter describes active sodium and chloride transport from the thick ascending limb into the medullary interstitium. This helps create a concentrated environment around the loop.

Water can then leave the water-permeable descending limb, concentrating the fluid that remains inside.

In the ascending limb, solute leaves while water is retained, so the tubular fluid becomes more dilute.

Finally, follow the fluid toward the collecting duct. Antidiuretic hormone increases water permeability there. More water returns to the body, and final urine volume decreases. Keep the two ideas separate: the loop builds the concentration gradient, while antidiuretic hormone regulates the final water adjustment. Motion, colors, and quantities are schematic.
