In the pulmonary system, an open alveolus provides a surface for gas exchange.

Type one alveolar cells provide structure. Type two cells secrete surfactant.

Notice the inner air surface during expiration. Surfactant lowers surface tension and helps prevent collapse.

Without enough surfactant, alveoli can collapse and ventilation falls. This is especially important in premature infants whose lungs have not developed adequate production and secretion.

Now follow oxygen across the alveolar and capillary walls. Diffusion depends on a thin membrane, a large surface area, and a partial pressure gradient. Blood flow then carries oxygen toward the tissues. Ventilation, diffusion, and perfusion are related, but they are different steps. A change in one step can disrupt exchange even when the others remain intact.
