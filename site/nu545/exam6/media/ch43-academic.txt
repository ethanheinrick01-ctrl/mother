Muscle contraction begins with electrical excitation. The supplied sequence reaches voltage-sensitive receptors in the transverse tubules and opens ryanodine channels. Released calcium enables actin to bind myosin.

Now watch thin filaments move inward relative to thick filaments. Their overlap increases and the sarcomere shortens. The motion represents cross-bridge activity, not shortening of the individual filaments. The packet identifies a continuing requirement for ATP and phosphocreatine.

During relaxation, calcium returns to the sarcoplasmic reticulum, cross-bridges detach, and the sarcomere lengthens. Follow the reverse motion. This film uses a textbook figure with schematic motion to separate excitation, coupling, contraction, and relaxation.
