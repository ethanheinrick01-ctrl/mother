Digestion needs both mixing and forward movement. In the small intestine, segmentation uses localized rhythmic contractions to divide and mix chyme against the absorbing surface. Watch neighboring sections narrow and relax while the contents remain in the same region.

Peristalsis serves a different purpose. A coordinated contraction wave moves the contents onward. Now the narrowing travels, and the bolus advances ahead of it.

Mixing improves contact; propulsion changes location. This distinction comes from Chapter forty, slide forty-six. The illustrated timing and motion are schematic. The source does not supply a complete motor-control pathway.
