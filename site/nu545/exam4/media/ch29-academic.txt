Normal clotting confines fibrin formation to a vessel injury. This scholarly repair figure provides the reference. Disseminated intravascular coagulation changes the location and extent of that process.

In DIC, widespread tissue factor exposure activates clotting. Thrombin and fibrin activity increase. Fibrin deposits inside small vessels impede blood flow, reducing organ perfusion.

As clotting continues, platelets and coagulation factors are consumed. Accelerated fibrinolysis accompanies the process. The same patient can therefore have microvascular thrombosis and uncontrolled bleeding.

Treatment goals include eliminating the underlying pathology, restoring hemostasis and supporting failing organs. Component replacement alone does not remove the trigger.
