The CDC figure compares a normal heart with a ventricular septal defect. The ventricular septum normally separates the two lower chambers. This institutional artwork is used for visualization, with no CDC endorsement of this study lab.

A ventricular septal defect leaves a communication. In the supplied postnatal pattern, higher left ventricular pressure sends additional blood toward the right ventricle and pulmonary circulation. Watch the added stream cross the septum.

A large shunt increases pulmonary flow. The chapter lists overcirculation and heart failure among possible consequences. This mechanism differs from an outflow obstruction or parallel circuits.

Pediatric heart failure can cause poor feeding, sweating and inadequate growth. Nutritional support addresses the growth problem; medication and corrective procedures address their stated targets.
