Heart failure is a supply problem with different mechanical routes. We use the same scholarly ventricular anatomy for both comparisons. The moving volume markers are schematic, not measured chamber volumes.

In systolic failure, impaired contraction reduces ejection. More blood remains after the beat, and stroke volume falls. The chapter connects reduced output with activation of compensatory systems.

In diastolic failure, relaxation and compliance are abnormal. Filling is restricted even when ejection fraction is preserved. A preserved fraction does not guarantee that enough blood enters and leaves the ventricle.

The slides distinguish pulmonary congestion in left failure from systemic venous congestion in right failure. Orthopnea and pulmonary edema belong to the pulmonary side; jugular venous distension and peripheral edema identify systemic congestion.
