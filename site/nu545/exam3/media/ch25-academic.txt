Start with the branching breast ducts and lobules. Now move closer to the duct wall. The basement membrane is the boundary that matters in this comparison.

Ductal carcinoma in situ is limited to the breast ducts and lobules without invading the basement membrane. Watch abnormal cells accumulate inside the boundary. Their number can increase while that boundary remains intact.

Invasive carcinoma crosses into surrounding tissue. Watch cells extend beyond the duct boundary in the second comparison. These are different disease states. The supplied slides do not establish that every in situ lesion will become invasive.

The released breast case then distinguishes a breast cancer deposit in the lung from a new primary lung cancer. A tumor found in the lung can still have a breast origin. Keep location, invasion, and tumor origin separate when interpreting the case.
