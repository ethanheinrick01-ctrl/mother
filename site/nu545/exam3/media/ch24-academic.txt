Follow the ovary and the uterine lining together. Rising follicle-stimulating hormone stimulates follicular growth. One follicle becomes dominant and produces increasing estradiol. Estrogen drives proliferation of the endometrium.

The luteinizing hormone surge causes ovulation. Watch the mature follicle release the ovum. Then follow the remaining follicle as it becomes the corpus luteum.

Luteinizing hormone stimulates the corpus luteum to secrete progesterone. Progesterone initiates the secretory phase. Endometrial glands and blood vessels branch and curl through the functional layer.

If implantation does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates. Estrogen and progesterone fall, and the endometrium degenerates and sheds. The hormone sequence explains the tissue sequence: follicular growth, ovulation, secretory change, and menstruation. Estrogen supports proliferation; progesterone initiates secretion.
