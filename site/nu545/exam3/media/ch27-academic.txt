Syphilis is caused by Treponema pallidum. In primary syphilis, focus on the local lesion: a firm, indurated, painless chancre, with firm, enlarged, nontender regional lymph nodes.

The supplied slides describe microorganisms draining with lymphatic fluid. Secondary syphilis has systemic manifestations, including low-grade fever, malaise, sore throat, generalized lymph-node enlargement, and skin or mucous-membrane lesions. Watch the local site connect with the wider lymphatic network.

Latent syphilis can be asymptomatic while medical evidence of infection remains. Absence of symptoms does not mean the infection has disappeared. Tertiary disease can include destructive gummas and neurosyphilis.

Keep the primary syphilitic chancre separate from chancroid. The supplied chancroid slide describes a painful, tender, soft chancre. Use the lesion findings and the disease stage together. This visual shows a schematic sequence, not a prediction for every patient.
